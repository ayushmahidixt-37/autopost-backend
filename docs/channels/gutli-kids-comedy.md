# Funny Tales — Show Bible v1

Channel name: **Funny Tales**
Show: "Nobody Believes Gutli" (Channel concept, dual-audience: kids + adults)
Status: Names and format confirmed. Episode length confirmed at Shorts (~60–75s). Next: prove the format with full sample scripts (see `funny-tales-episode-scripts.md`), then move to voice/character production assets.

## Logline
A 6-year-old and her sarcastic stuffed bear best friend — who only comes alive for her — cause daily chaos that the grown-ups never quite believe.

## Core cast

### Baba (the kid)
- Age 6. Curious, stubborn, fiercely loyal to Gutli, always the one who gets blamed.
- Not naive — she knows exactly how ridiculous this looks to adults, which is the source of most of the adult-facing humor (her exasperated "looks at camera" moments).
- Default emotional state: mildly done with today's nonsense, but never actually mad at Gutli.

### Gutli (the best friend)
- A patchwork stuffed bear: mismatched button eyes (one slightly higher than the other), one floppy ear, a lopsided hand-stitched smile, no clothing/accessories.
- **Deliberately not styled like any existing bear IP** — no duffel coat/hat (not Paddington), no red shirt/honey motif (not Pooh), no bowtie or human-passing design (not Ted). His whole visual identity is "clearly homemade, slightly worn, one-of-a-kind."
- Personality: deadpan, unbothered, dry wit, terrible ideas delivered with total confidence. Secretly very loyal — the ideas are bad, the intentions are good.
- Voice: low-energy, sarcastic, monotone even mid-chaos. Never yells.

### World rule (the engine of every episode)
Gutli is fully alive and talks freely — but **only Baba can see/hear him animated.** To every adult and every other character, he's just an ordinary stuffed bear sitting perfectly still. This is what generates the format forever: Baba tries to explain what Gutli did, the adult sees only a motionless toy and a kid telling a wild story.

## Why this works for both audiences
- Kids get: silly voice, physical slapstick, a magical secret best friend.
- Adults get: the dramatic irony of watching the adult characters completely misread the situation, plus Gutli's dry delivery — same joke, two read levels, no separate content needed.

## Episode format template (Shorts-length, ~60–75s)
1. **Cold open (0–3s)** — drop into the aftermath: "Baba, why is there ketchup on the ceiling?"
2. **Setup (3–15s)** — quick flashback to Gutli's idea.
3. **Escalation (15–40s)** — the plan snowballs.
4. **Punchline (40–55s)** — Gutli's deadpan line + visual payoff.
5. **Blame beat (55–65s)** — adult remains oblivious; Baba gets blamed; knowing look to camera.
6. **Outro tag (65–75s)** — recurring sign-off line (see below) for branding/recognition.

## Recurring devices (for consistency + algorithm recognition)
- Gutli's catchphrase after every disaster: **"...Worth it."**
- Baba's running line: **"Gutli, we talked about this."**
- Visual gag: Gutli instantly frozen in an innocent sitting pose the moment an adult looks over.
- Consistent outro card/sound for channel branding.

## Sample episode premises (proves the format scales)
1. Gutli declares the blanket fort an independent nation.
2. Gutli "helps" with the school science project; it becomes sentient slime.
3. Gutli insists there's a monster under the bed and they must befriend it with cookies — there is, and it's friendly.
4. Gutli organizes a 2am backyard "Toy Olympics."
5. Gutli decides the vacuum cleaner is the enemy; resistance is organized.
6. Gutli "fixes" a broken lamp with tape, glitter, and confidence.
7. Gutli appoints himself translator for the family dog, inventing increasingly absurd demands.
8. Gutli sets up a "guard rotation" with booby traps for baby brother's nap time.

## Visual style direction (for template-based production)
- Flat, rounded, simple-shape 2D style (or a soft felt/plush texture treatment) — chosen for reusability, not just aesthetics: a small library of swappable background templates (bedroom, living room, backyard, kitchen) plus a fixed character rig for Baba and Gutli lets the content-generation step compose new episodes without custom animation per video.
- Limited, consistent color palette across episodes for brand recognition.

## Content safety / compliance notes
- No realistic danger modeled — all mishaps stay cartoonish/exaggerated, never a real-world hazard a kid could imitate (no real climbing risk, no real chemicals, etc.).
- Every episode resolves positively — Baba and Gutli are never actually at odds, no bullying, no lasting harm.
- This is a **child-directed show** — plan for the "Made for Kids" classification regardless of adult viewership (see PROJECT_SUMMARY.md, compliance is content-based, not audience-based).
- These rules should become literal checklist items for the automated Review/QA agent before anything publishes.
